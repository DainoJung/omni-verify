## LinkedIn

(Type "@TestMu AI" and pick the company page from the dropdown so the tag is real. Attach both images.)

Marketing promises and the real product drift apart silently. AI growth agents make it worse: they write promises at scale, and they learn from the numbers.

I'm a solo founder. An AI agent writes and posts marketing for my app, Scribo, and I judge what works from tracking tags. Today at Kane CLI Hack Day by @TestMu AI, I found two ways this goes wrong, both on my own app.

Drift 1, the link lies. Every App Store button on my site overwrites the campaign tag. Every install from my Threads posts would have counted as a website install. No error, just a wrong number, and my agent would have concluded Threads doesn't work.

Drift 2, the product moves on. I rebuilt Scribo this month, from a handwriting vocabulary app into a content learning app. My older posts still promise the old app, and they keep bringing people in.

So I built Omni Verify. The agent drafts a post and turns every promise into a check. Kane CLI walks each one in a real browser before the post ships, and again after every release. Failed promises get repaired from what Kane saw, and the agent remembers which promises are retired. Only a post that passes can publish.

It's real: this Threads post went out through the gate today, and Publish stayed locked until Kane passed that exact version.
https://www.threads.com/@scribo.voca/post/De4-yPnATF5

What a PASS doesn't mean: it doesn't mean installs went up. It means the post tells the truth when it ships.

AI can write the promise. Kane checks if the product keeps it.

Repo and every Kane run: https://github.com/DainoJung/omni-verify
